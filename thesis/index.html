Footprints Before the News

1 People who know first leave footprints before the news, in volume, options and prediction-market bets, and when those footprints line up across linked markets, the move that follows beats what options priced in.

This page is the thesis and the tracker that tests it. A GitHub Action reruns the tracker after every US close, so the numbers below stay current.

At a glance

1. ThesisInformed traders hide in the price but not in the volume, so footprints show up before the news.
2. Time horizonThe edge should last about 1–2 years (until enforcement catches up). Trades last 15–60 minutes for minute bursts and 1–5 days (about 3) for daily flags.
3. Tracker41 assets scored nightly on quiet volume, breaks from the pack, minute bursts, options flow and greeks, and prediction-market jumps and odd bettors. The Footprint Index counts assets scoring 2+, and the web shows today's moves across the market.
4. Wrong if(1) Moves after flags don't beat what options priced. (2) Minute bursts follow through no more often than scheduled news. (3) Footprints show up after the headline, not before.
5. Alpha / costRoughly 0.3–0.8% gross per flagged trade over 1–5 days, and 0.2–0.6% net in stock. Options cost 2–5% of premium each way.
6. MacroIt works because 2026 is a headline market. Geopolitics and regulation matter most, the Fed less, and selloffs push correlations up.

Why I believe it

The clearest examples come from this spring. On March 23, about $500M in oil futures were sold in the 15 minutes before the President posted that strikes on Iran's energy infrastructure would be delayed, and oil fell about 14% within minutes. On April 7, about $950M in bets against oil went in hours before the two-week ceasefire. On April 17, $760M in Brent sell orders hit about 20 minutes before Iran's foreign minister said the Strait of Hormuz would stay open. The CFTC and DOJ are now investigating these trades.

The key detail: in one of these cases, $430M of sell orders only moved Brent from $100.91 to $100.66. A minute after the announcement it was at $96.83. The informed side kept the price quiet, but the volume gave it away. That's the "quiet volume" footprint this tracker looks for.

Prediction markets show the same pattern. Before the Iran strike and the ceasefire, dozens of brand-new Polymarket wallets placed large, accurate bets hours ahead, and a Harvard study estimates about $143M in Polymarket profits may have come from inside information.

The Nvidia story in November shows the second idea: markets are a web. When the board opened its investigation on Sunday, November 8, Nvidia fell while Asian chip suppliers rallied. When Jensen Huang resigned on November 12, the shock spread to TSM and MU. News about one node moves its neighbors, so a neighbor moving before the news is a clue.

Academic support: Pan & Poteshman (2006) found that stocks with low put-call ratios beat stocks with high ones by more than 40bp the next day and more than 1% over the next week. That predictive power came from option volume that wasn't public, so it is evidence that informed traders show up in volume first. It also warns that public versions of the signal are weaker.

Option prices leak information too, not just volume. Cremers & Weinbaum (2010) found that stocks with relatively expensive calls beat stocks with relatively expensive puts by about 50bp a week. Xing, Zhang & Zhao (2010) found that stocks with the steepest put smirks underperformed by about 10.9% a year, consistent with informed traders who have bad news buying out-of-the-money puts. That's the basis for the greeks layer.

Case study: Jensen Huang's return (Nov 17–23)

This is the cleanest example of the whole thesis, and it happened while I was writing it. On Tuesday the board said publicly it would not bring Huang back, and Polymarket gave a return by November 30 about 6% odds. Six days later he was CEO again. In between, footprints showed up in every market the tracker watches:

Two lessons went straight into the tracker. First, the options market showed the bet before the stock did: calls were piling up while the price kept falling. So options flow is now a footprint, with extra attention when it leans against the stock's own move. Second, on prediction markets the odds barely moved before the news. The tell was who was betting: a wallet that had been asleep for a year, an Iran-war bettor and a sports bettor all piling into the same longshot. So the tracker now checks the history of every wallet that puts $5K or more into a linked market. There's also a third lesson from the options side: buying that much near-term upside while the stock falls usually lifts front-month implied vol and makes calls expensive relative to puts, which is exactly what the greeks layer looks for.

It also matches the timing you'd expect: positions built Friday, the decision made over the weekend (the President said he spoke with Huang on Sunday after the board voted), and the announcement before Monday's open. The spring oil trades followed a similar before-the-weekend pattern, so the tracker tags Friday footprints.

2Time horizon

How long the thesis should work: about 1 to 2 years. It depends on three things that are true right now. Policy keeps moving markets through sudden posts and leaks, prediction markets are growing faster than anyone can police them, and enforcement is slow. The CFTC and DOJ probes and the new prediction-market bills are the clock on this edge. If they start catching people, the footprints get smaller.

How long each trade lasts depends on the footprint:

  • Minute bursts: 15 to 60 minutes. The oil trades paid off within half an hour. A nightly job can't catch these live, so for now they're evidence that raises conviction on the next day's trade. If Sanji can take intraday orders later, these become their own trades.
  • Daily confluence (score ≥ 2): 1 to 5 trading days, about 3 on average. That matches the window where Pan & Poteshman found option volume still predicted returns.
  • Slow positioning: weeks to months. Someone who knows early may build a position slowly. The tracker keeps every burst and odds jump in an archive (events.json, pm_log.json), so we can look back at what happened before each big move.

3The tracker

Definition. Every weekday after the close, each of 41 assets (indexes, Treasuries, the dollar, oil, gas, gold, silver, copper, bitcoin, the Mag 7, semis, energy, defense and airlines) gets a footprint score:

  • Quiet volume (+1): daily volume at least 2 standard deviations above its 20-day normal, while the price move stays under 1 standard deviation.
  • Break from the pack (+1): each asset's move is predicted from its 4 closest neighbors over the last 60 days. If the miss is bigger than 2.5 standard deviations, it broke away.
  • Minute burst (+1): a 1-minute bar with at least 8x normal volume (robust z ≥ 4) and almost no price change. Bursts within 3 minutes of known public times (8:30 data, 10:30 oil inventories, settlements, the open and close, 2pm FOMC) don't count.
  • Options flow (+1): on 18 option-heavy names, contracts expiring in 5–45 days (this week's contracts are skipped, since their volume beats open interest every day) trading at least 1.5x their open interest, so mostly brand-new positions. It only flags once a name has 8 nights of its own history, and then the new-position dollars have to be unusual for that name specifically. "Against the tape" marks flow that leans the opposite way to the stock's move.
  • Greeks and the vol surface (counts with options flow; +1.5 if both fire): greeks are computed for every contract with Black-Scholes, using implied vols solved from each contract's own price (Yahoo's IV column is too noisy). It's a footprint if: (a) front-month ATM implied vol jumps 10%+ on a day the stock barely moved; (b) the front month sits 10+ vol points over the ~35-day month with no earnings in between, meaning someone is paying for an event nobody has announced; or (c) skew shifts 3+ vol points overnight, either calls getting expensive versus puts (bullish) or puts getting expensive versus at-the-money calls (bearish). After 8 nights of history, (a) and (c) use z-scores instead.
  • Prediction markets (+1): either a linked Polymarket or Kalshi market moves at least 8 points within an hour, or someone puts $5K+ into a linked market at 60¢ or less (betting against the crowd) from a wallet that's brand new, has been dormant 30+ days, or usually bets on something else entirely (for example, a sports bettor's first Nvidia bet), or Kalshi sees 5+ trades over $3K in a day. Iran questions link to oil, Fed questions to Treasuries, CEO questions to the stock and its suppliers. Markets about to settle, same-day markets and sports/esports are ignored.
  • Upstream (+0.5): an asset that normally moves the day before this one (the arrows in the web below) had a footprint today.

An asset is flagged at a score of 2 or more. The Footprint Index is how many assets are flagged each day.

The web: who moves with whom

A market heat map in web form. Each bubble is an asset, colored by today's move: green up, red down, darker for bigger, measured against the move options priced for the day (or the usual daily move when there are no options). Each sector keeps its own spot, so the map looks the same every day and only the colors change. Lines join each asset to its (up to) 4 closest partners, correlated above 0.55 over the last 60 sessions. A line stays gray unless both ends really moved today; then it turns green or red. A purple dashed line means the two went opposite ways on heavy volume, a hint that money rotated from one to the other. The sentence on top is written automatically from the same data. Drag bubbles, drag the background to pan, scroll to zoom, and click a bubble to pin its details.

Minute bursts (unscheduled)

"30m move" is the biggest move in the next 30 minutes. "Follow" is that move divided by a normal 30-minute move; 2x or more counts as followed through. "Linked" lists other markets that burst within 5 minutes.

Prediction markets

Polymarket and Kalshi markets that map to the universe, sorted by the biggest one-hour move in the last day.

Options flow: new positions tonight

"New-position $" is premium traded in contracts whose volume beat 1.5x their open interest. "Lean" runs from -1 (all puts) to +1 (all calls), counting a contract as bought when its last trade printed at or above the mid. Closing quotes can't prove who was buying, so treat the lean as a hint.

Greeks and the vol surface

Front IV: at-the-money implied vol for the first expiry 5+ days out (overnight change in brackets). Term: front minus ~35-day IV; positive means people are paying for something soon. Call−put: call IV minus put IV at the same strikes, weighted by open interest (Cremers & Weinbaum); positive means calls are in demand. Smirk: 95% put IV minus at-the-money call IV (Xing, Zhang & Zhao); rising means crash protection is in demand. New Δ: dollar delta of tonight's new positions, signed by bought or sold, and as a share of the stock's dollar volume. New vega: dollars gained per vol point. Dealer Γ: dealer gamma per 1% move, assuming customers are long calls and puts. Negative means dealers have to chase moves, so moves get bigger. That doesn't change the score, but it helps size the bet.

Who's betting: out-of-place wallets

Wallets that put $5K or more into a linked Polymarket market in the last 48 hours, with what they usually do. Polymarket trades are public on-chain; Kalshi accounts are anonymous, so Kalshi only shows bursts of $3K+ trades.

4I'm wrong if…

Each check updates live from the tracker. "Not enough data" means the sample is still too small to judge.

5Rough alpha and costs

Gross edge. Pan & Poteshman's informed-volume signal earned about 40bp a day and 1% a week, but that came from data that wasn't public. My public version will be weaker. My rough guess: flagged trades capture 0.3% to 0.8% per trade over 1 to 5 days on liquid stocks and ETFs, with a hit rate a little above a coin flip (about 55%). Most flags will be noise, like someone placing one big dumb order. The edge comes from the few real ones being much bigger than normal.

Greeks. Cremers & Weinbaum's call-minus-put spread earned about 50bp a week in the original study, but they also found the effect shrank over their sample as markets learned. I treat the greeks layer as a way to confirm direction and size the bet, not as a separate source of profit. A negative dealer gamma reading says that if the news lands, the move will probably overshoot, which favors options over stock.

Costs. Stocks and ETFs like SPY, NVDA and USO cost about 1 to 3bp per side in spread, so costs barely matter there. Short-dated options on TSLA, NVDA or MSFT cost much more: a 2% to 5% spread on premium each way, plus time decay if the move takes longer than expected. Options give more payoff on the rare big move, so they fit this thesis, but only if the move ratio below stays well above 1.

Net. In stock, roughly 0.2% to 0.6% per flagged trade after costs. In options it could be more or it could be negative, depending on how fast moves follow. The live check replaces my guess with data:

6Macro environment

The thesis works because 2026 is a headline market. Big moves come from decisions a small group knows about before everyone else: war and ceasefire calls, tariffs, and CEO exits. Liquid futures and prediction markets let that group trade large size fast, and while they can hide the price impact, they can't hide the volume. Geopolitics and oil matter most. Most of the best evidence came from the Iran conflict, so if it calms down, there will be fewer secrets worth trading and fewer real flags. The dollar is the best clue to which kind of day it is. On rates-driven days (strong data, a tough-sounding Fed), yields and the dollar rise together, dollar/yen rises, and gold and silver fall. On fear-driven days (war, shocks), money runs to Treasuries, so yields fall while the dollar still rises, gold often rises with it, and oil jumps if supply is at risk. The links in the web flip between those two kinds of day, which is why the web is rebuilt every night instead of being fixed. The Fed matters less than you'd think: FOMC decisions are scheduled and leaks are rare during the blackout period, so FOMC minutes are excluded and Fed odds on Kalshi and Polymarket are only a signal when they jump outside scheduled data. Lower rates and risk-on markets raise volume everywhere, which makes quiet-volume flags noisier. A tech selloff like the one after Huang's exit pushes correlations up, so everything moves together. That makes a real break rarer and more meaningful, but it floods the volume side with false flags. That's why the web is rebuilt every night on a rolling 60-day window. Finally, regulation is the biggest risk to the edge. If the CFTC/DOJ probes or prediction-market bills start catching people, the footprints should shrink, and wrong-if #3 watches for exactly that.
