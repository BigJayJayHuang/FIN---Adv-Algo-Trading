Summary

The wheel vs buy & hold

Same stock, same window, both starting from the wheel's own required capital (100 shares' worth of cash, fully cash-secured - see write-up). The only question that matters: did selling premium every week beat just owning the stock?

NAV over time

Both lines start at the same dollar amount, so this reads directly as dollars, not just percent.

Blotter

Every trade that actually happened: each week's put or call sold, plus every open, close, assignment, and roll. No working orders, no signals, nothing hypothetical.

Week by week

One row per expiry Friday: whether it was a put (flat) or call (holding) week, the vol regime or cost-basis tier that set the target strike, the strike actually sold, and how the week ended.

    Account

    The exact portfolio at any point in time: cash, shares, whatever option is currently short (put or call) and its mark, and NAV, at every bar of the backtest.

    Rows

    Mid vs trade

    Every bar where a short option - put or call, whichever leg was open that week - both had a real bid/ask and a trade printed. If trades sit on the line, mid is a fair price to assume for entry and early-close fills. Y is TRDPRC_1, X is (BID+ASK)/2, same bar.

    Checks

    Re-derived independently from the raw bars, not from the engine's own bookkeeping. If one fails it shows here.
