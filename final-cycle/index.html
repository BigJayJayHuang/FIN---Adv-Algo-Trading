Sleeper / Striker

A nightly options algorithm. When the options tape is quiet it sells premium (Sleeper). When somebody starts placing irregular, one-sided bets it switches modes and follows them (Striker). Every night it writes open_orders.json for Sanji.

Why this algo

An algorithm is a function that takes inputs and returns a set of open orders. I wanted that function to do two different jobs, because options markets behave in two different ways:

Most nights: nothing is happening

Implied vol usually sits above the vol the stock actually realizes. That gap (the volatility risk premium) is the reason my HW3 wheel and the covered calls in HW1-2 made money. On a quiet night the best trade is to sell some of that premium with capped risk.

Some nights: someone knows something

Every so often a lot of volume lands on strikes that had little open interest, gets filled at the ask, and all leans the same way. That is someone opening a large new position. Selling premium into that is how theta sellers get run over. It's better to stop and trade in the same direction.

So the algo is a regime switch. Sleeper is the calm default: it sells credit spreads sized off the expected move. Striker is the aggressive mode: it buys options in the direction of the irregular flow, sized by how many signals agree. The options greeks (delta, gamma, vega) appear in three places: they weight the flow, they measure dealer positioning (gamma exposure), and they choose between a naked long option and a debit spread when vega is expensive.

I trade TSLA and NVDA because they're volatile, have very liquid weekly options, and carry a lot of retail and institutional flow, so irregular orders actually show up in them. MSFT stays in the book as the fallback. The course server only prices tickers that have bars in my price_history.json, so until TSLA and NVDA have bars the algo still scans them but only places orders on MSFT.

Step 2: the scanner (final-cycle/signals.py)

The scanner looks at contracts expiring in 1-30 days with strikes within ±25% of spot. For each contract it computes the mid, Black-Scholes delta / gamma / vega / theta from that contract's IV, and the dollar premium traded (volume × mid × 100).

Step 3: the decision (signals.decide)

STRIKER  follow the flow

Requires checks 1 and 2, plus at least 3 of the 5 in total:

  1. Irregular flow: ≥ 20% of premium and ≥ 3 contracts
  2. Flow is one-sided: |flow score| ≥ 0.35
  3. IV popped: ATM IV up ≥ 8% vs last night
  4. Dealers short gamma: GEX < 0
  5. Price agrees: 5-day return has the same sign as the flow

Size: 3 points → 2 contracts, 4 → 3, 5 → 5.
Trade: buy the call (or put) about ¼ expected move out of the money on the 5DTE+ expiry. If IV is already rich (IV rank ≥ 80, or IV/RV ≥ 1.6 before there's history), also sell a further OTM option at 1 expected move, making it a debit spread that's less exposed to an IV crush.

SLEEPER  sell theta

All five must pass:

  1. Quiet tape: the irregular-flow check did not fire
  2. Flow not leaning against the spread
  3. IV rich: ATM IV − RV20 ≥ 3 vol points
  4. Dealers not short gamma: GEX ≥ -0.15
  5. No earnings within 10 days

Trade: above the 20-day average, a bull put spread: sell the put 1 expected move below, buy the put 1.8 expected moves below. Below it, the mirror-image bear call spread. 1 contract, or 2 when VRP ≥ 8 points.

IDLE  stand aside

Two cases. Heavy irregular flow with no clear direction (for example someone buying straddles): something is brewing, so selling premium is dangerous and there's no side to follow. Or a quiet tape with no edge in the premium.

Step 4: managing the book (book.manage)

  • Anything with ≤ 1 day to expiry is closed.
  • Any position is closed if a Striker signal fires the other way on that ticker.
  • Striker longs: take profit at +100%, stop at -50%.
  • Sleeper spreads: close once 50% of the credit is kept, or if spot crosses the short strike.
  • Follow-ups: sell the short leg against a Striker long once it's filled, and re-try a Sleeper short that didn't fill (details under Orders).
  • Only one idea per ticker at a time. Until Sanji publishes positions.json, a ledger of what was sent (with fills.json once it exists) stops stacking.

Orders: pricing ourselves

Each order names an exact contract picked from tonight's chain: a numeric strike and a calendar expiry, chosen the way Sanji's pct: and 5DTE+ would pick them. Most orders also carry a numeric limit price. Sanji resolves bare numbers without an option-chain snapshot, which he doesn't have yet. mid-0.05-style limits and delta: strikes would be rejected until he does.

How Sanji fills a limit, and how we choose ours

A limit buy fills at the open if the open is already at or below the limit; otherwise it fills at the limit if the day's low trades through it. Sells mirror that with the high. So for every limit, final-cycle/pricing.py simulates 3,000 versions of tomorrow from tonight's close: an overnight gap, then the session in 15-minute steps, with the option valued by Black-Scholes along each path. That gives a distribution of the option's open, high and low, and for every candidate price we count how often Sanji would fill it. We keep the best price that still meets a fill target.

The tilt: 30% of today's move is assumed to carry into tomorrow. If the stock sold off today and we're buying calls, the paths drift lower, the option's likely low is lower, and the bid comes down with it. If the stock is already running our way, the bid goes up instead of missing the move.

Legging rule: a short option never goes out on its own

  • Sleeper (if switched to limits): the wing is a market buy (always fills) and the short is a limit. Worst case the short doesn't fill and we hold a cheap wing, which the next night re-tries the short against or sells.
  • Striker debit spread: only the long goes out. Once positions.json shows we own it, the short is sold against it.
  • Closing a spread patiently covers the short first. The long left behind is handled the next night. Urgent closes send every leg at market.
  • Every order set passes a naked_risk check before it's written.

Testing & backtest

Free sources don't keep historical option chains with volume and OI, so I can't backtest this on real flow. Instead I built a test market (final-cycle/sim.py): a stock path plus a full option chain every night, with irregular days planted on purpose. The backtest then runs the exact nightly functions on it and fills the orders the way Sanji describes, on each option's intraday bar. Market orders (and limits that fill right at the open) pay half the bid/ask. A resting limit that the day's low or high trades through fills at the limit. Plus $0.65 a contract.

How to read this. In the test market the informed flow predicts the move by construction, so these returns prove the plumbing (detection, sizing, orders, fills, exits), not that the strategy makes money in real markets. What carries over is the detection result: planted bets get caught in the right direction, and noise, straddles and IV-only spikes don't fire Striker. Whether real irregular flow is informative is exactly what the nightly run will start to show.

An honest result about Sleeper: it wins about three trades in four, but the losers are bigger and the bid/ask on four fills per spread eats the rest, so on its own it's slightly negative here. Raising the VRP bar, moving the strikes further out, or holding to expiry all landed within a percent of break-even. Striker drives the returns. Sleeper is the low-risk default, and the live log will show whether real-world VRP (which is wider than this test market's) pays for it. One switch (sleeper_enabled in signals.Config) turns the algo into Striker-only.

Unit tests (final-cycle/tests/test_algo.py, 40 tests, run before every nightly run)

  • Greeks: put-call parity, delta ordering, IV solver round-trip.
  • Hand-built chains with known answers: a call sweep at the ask is bullish; the same contracts hit at the bid are not; a put sweep is bearish; straddle buying is irregular but stands aside; an IV spike alone never strikes; one big print isn't enough; quiet + rich IV sells a put spread; empty chains and missing IVs don't crash.
  • Planted scenarios × 12 seeds: normal nights never strike; informed nights are always seen (≥ 3 irregular contracts, flow sign correct), ≥ 75% strike and never the wrong way; hedge noise is flagged but at most 1 in 12 strikes; IV-only nights never strike.
  • Sanji: the spec's own example validates; 10 kinds of illegal order are rejected; the token resolver matches the spec wording (ATM ties go lower, puts OTM is down, 5DTE+/5DTE-/W+/M+).
  • Orders: every structure builds legal orders on exact contracts, under both pricing modes; no short option ever goes out without its protection (a limit wing doesn't count).
  • Pricing: Sanji's fill rule (open already through, or the day's low/high trading through); a higher fill target pays more but never above the ask; a stock sliding into the open gets a lower bid than one rising; tick rounding; each Striker style (market, dip, protected, split) behaves as described.
  • Managing the book: take-profit goes out as a limit, urgent exits at market; a credit spread covers its short first; the leg-in short is sold only against a long we own; an orphan wing re-tries its short or gets sold; positions.json read in several shapes.
  • Calendar & end-to-end: Monday night → Tuesday, Wednesday before Thanksgiving → Friday, Friday → Monday; the whole nightly job on demo data writes a valid open_orders.json.

Limits & what I'd add next

  • Aggressor is estimated from the last print versus the bid/ask. Real flow tools use every trade. With LSEG time-and-sales this would be much sharper.
  • Thresholds are set on the test market. The nightly page logs the raw irregular share, flow score and IV, so after a few weeks they can be tuned on real nights.
  • Delayed, end-of-day data. The orders fill at tomorrow's open, so a whole night of news sits between signal and fill.
  • Things to ask for in Sanji: delta: strikes and mid±x limits (both need chain snapshots; a limit relative to the open would adjust for the overnight gap), and multi-leg orders so a spread fills as one unit. That would let Sleeper use limits safely.
  • The fill model runs optimistic (see the calibration table). It could learn a correction from the live fills.json once Sanji is resolving orders.
