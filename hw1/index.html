Write-up

Calls show a real cluster of prices when the strike is close to where the stock is trading and the expiration is coming up soon, but that cloud thins out fast the further you get from the money or the further out the expiration goes. Puts resolved this time: 195 of 240 candidate put contracts came back with real LSEG data, against 218 of 240 on the call side. The density pattern looks the same on both sides -- strikes close to the money with an expiration coming up soon are the ones with real quotes, thinning out fast the further you get from either. An earlier version of this pipeline generated put RICs with the wrong expired-contract suffix (the letter after the ^ has to be that month's call letter even on a put -- the base symbol's own letter is what already encodes put-vs-call), so every put request came back empty regardless of whether real data existed. Fixing that suffix turned this from a parsing bug into an ordinary, explainable gap. You can't fill in a gap on a $0.50 strike grid by guessing what's between two real prices when there aren't two real prices to begin with -- where there's nothing on either side of a gap, any number put there wouldn't be an estimate, it would just be made up. MID_PRICE is the price actually used to value a position; TRDPRC_1 is proof someone actually traded at that price -- keeping those two separate, rather than papering over whichever one is missing, is the point of this view.
